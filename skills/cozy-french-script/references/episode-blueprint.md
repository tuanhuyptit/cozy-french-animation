# The Cozy French — Episode blueprint

Built from an analysis of the two biggest English-learning podcast channels (Nov 2026) and adapted to our slower French format. Read this before every script.

## 1. Length and pace

| | Competitors (English) | The Cozy French |
|---|---|---|
| Speaking rate | ~200 words/min | ~135 words/min (slow French) |
| Episode | 4 000–5 500 words, 16–37 min | **2 400–2 700 words, 18–19 min** |

So a competitor script is always COMPRESSED, never followed line by line. Keep their best beats, drop tangents and repeated summaries.

## 2. The 11 blocks (word budget ≈ total 2 600)

| # | Block (header to write) | Words | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `# 1. Accroche` | 60–90 | 4–6 quick lines BEFORE the welcome: a funny question or confession tied to the topic (e.g. Mathieu's "voyage" from the sofa to bed via the cheese). Ends on a punchline. |
| 2 | `# 2. Ouverture` | 120–160 | "Bonjour/Bonsoir tout le monde et bienvenue sur The Cozy French." Names. "Où que vous soyez…" (at home, in the métro, on a break). Topic in one sentence. Level ("Cette conversation est pour les niveaux B1–B2"). Promise: "À la fin de cet épisode, vous saurez…" (2–3 concrete skills). Reassurance: "Pas besoin de comprendre chaque mot." |
| 3 | `# 3. Petit rendez-vous` | 50–80 | Community moment woven into the conversation, not a dry "like & subscribe": invite listeners to leave a ☕ in the comments if they understood a joke without translating, or to say where they are listening from. Then "the question to keep in mind" for the episode. |
| 4 | `# 4. Histoire de Léa` | 350–450 | A concrete personal story with sensory details (time, place, objects). Mathieu reacts, teases, asks questions. 2–3 vocab items explained inline. |
| 5 | `# 5. Histoire de Mathieu` | 300–400 | Mathieu's contrasting story (often the funny "bad example"). 2–3 vocab items. |
| 6 | `# 6. Pourquoi c'est difficile` | 250–350 | The emotional insight: why we really do this (e.g. "le soir, c'est le seul moment où personne ne nous demande rien"). Calm, sincere, 1–2 vocab items. This is what makes viewers comment. |
| 7 | `# 7. Jeu de rôle` | 250–320 | "On essaie ? Je suis ta voisine, il est tard…" Announce the scene, ONE line of the situation, then stop: "À vous ! Qu'est-ce que vous diriez ?" (leave the next line as a short pause sentence like "Prenez quelques secondes."). Then play the full mini-dialogue, then 1–2 lines on why it works. |
| 8 | `# 8. Des solutions simples` | 300–400 | 2–3 practical, small, realistic ideas, each tied to a story detail. Mathieu tests one and fails a bit, Léa adjusts it. 2–3 vocab items. |
| 9 | `# 9. À vous de parler` | 150–220 | A sentence frame ("Avant de me coucher, je… Ça m'aide à…"), one example from each host, then "Dites votre phrase à voix haute." |
| 10 | `# 10. Le petit tour des mots` | 300–380 | Spoken recap of 5 key expressions. For each: remind the moment in the story → simple definition → one NEW example → "On répète : …". Optional mini "Dites-le autrement": 2–3 simple sentences re-said with a natural French expression. |
| 11 | `# 11. Au revoir` | 120–170 | Back to the block-9 frame + add a reason ("parce que…"). Comment question with a fill-in template ("Écrivez : « Le soir, j'aime… parce que… »"). "Abonnez-vous à The Cozy French". Warm sign-off ("Prenez soin de vous et à très bientôt !"). LAST 2–4 lines: callback to the block-1 joke. |

Rhythm rules: change speaker every 1–3 sentences; a light joke or a funny image at least every ~90 seconds (≈ 200 words); never more than 3 lines of pure explanation in a row.

## 3. Vocabulary
- 13–16 items per episode, level B1–B2, mostly expressions and verbs used in daily life (se changer, avoir du mal à, perdre le fil, au cas où, tomber de sommeil…). Avoid A1 words (manger, maison) and rare C1 literary words.
- Pattern: in context → reaction or question ("Décompresser ?") → simple French definition ("ça veut dire…", "c'est quand…") → new example sentence (not from the story).
- Space them out: at least ~45 words between two items (the video shows a card for each word).

## 4. French adaptation — change the world, not just the words

| Competitor (US/UK) | Cozy French equivalent |
|---|---|
| Anna & Jake, Emily & Paul | Léa & Mathieu (never other host names) |
| Coffee to go, Starbucks, takeout | Café au comptoir, la boulangerie, un plat à emporter, le marché du dimanche |
| Commute by car, highway | Métro, RER, tram, vélo, le train de 7 h 42 |
| Dollars, pounds, miles, °F | Euros, kilomètres, degrés Celsius |
| 3 p.m., 11 p.m. | 15 heures, 23 heures, "vers minuit" |
| Lunch at the desk (20 min) | La pause déjeuner (vraie coupure), la cantine, le resto du coin |
| Thanksgiving, 4th of July | Le 14 juillet, Noël en famille, la galette des rois, les vacances d'août, la rentrée de septembre |
| Shops open on Sunday | Le dimanche, presque tout est fermé (sauf la boulangerie le matin) |
| Doctor / GP | Le médecin traitant, la pharmacie du quartier |
| Roommates, landlord | Colocation, le propriétaire, le voisin du dessus, la gardienne de l'immeuble |
| Small talk about weather | Aussi la météo, mais encore plus : la grève, les vacances, ce qu'on a mangé |
| Hug hello | La bise (and the eternal question: deux ou trois ?) |
| School: high school, college | Le lycée, le bac, la fac |

Avoid clichés (béret, baguette sous le bras à chaque phrase, Tour Eiffel). Cities may be named occasionally (Lyon, Nantes, Bordeaux, Paris) but most stories happen "dans mon quartier", "chez moi", "au bureau".

## 5. Tone of Léa and Mathieu (running jokes to reuse)
- Mathieu: the fridge and the cheese, his old slippers "qui connaissent tous ses secrets", tasks that turn into investigations, loses his keys/glasses, very polite to objects ("les carottes restent très patientes").
- Léa: precise, organised, but also has funny weak points (an album that becomes a museum, a shelf tidy that ends with all books on the floor). She deserves "une grande assiette, même pour une omelette".
- Humour is gentle and self-mocking, never mean, never about nationalities.

## 6. Output format reminder
```
# 1. Accroche
LÉA: Mathieu, petite question.
MATHIEU: Oui ?

# 2. Ouverture
LÉA: Bonsoir tout le monde et bienvenue sur The Cozy French…
```
No brackets, no emotion tags, no stage directions. Emotion = words + punctuation.
